# Avatar Deep Dive Research

## Pain Points
- "My face is darker than my body. Do you have any ideas or advice on how to lighten my face so it matches my body? Please recommend some products or things I can do to fix this problem. 🥹"
Insight: The crying emoji is doing significant emotional work here. This isn't a clinical inquiry — it's a plea. The avatar feels helpless, slightly embarrassed, and is reaching out to a community because professional or personal support hasn't been enough.
- "I'm honestly at a bad place mentally because I'm so confused by how I look. Indoors I think my skin looks fine where my skin is all the same colour with no spots, but when I'm outside in natural lighting and try to take selfies I just see every dark spot on my face, I get insecure and disgusted by my skin and it's getting to a point where I'm starting to have face dysmorphia."
Insight: This is the rawest data point in the entire set. The avatar isn't just unhappy with their skin — they are experiencing psychological distress. The word "disgusted" reveals a deep shame response triggered by a reflection they can't control. The gap between indoor and outdoor perception creates an unstable self-image that makes them feel like they can't trust their own eyes.
- "I'm being conscious about this now so how to get rid of this."
Insight: The word "conscious" is a turning point word — it signals the moment this problem crossed from background noise into foreground identity threat. Once someone becomes "conscious," the issue is no longer cosmetic; it affects how they move through the world.
- "this makes me feel ugly and I could talk to anyone confidently."
Insight: The grammatical slip ("could" instead of "couldn't") doesn't dilute the emotional weight — it actually amplifies it. This is someone typing in genuine distress. Uneven skin tone has directly collapsed their social confidence. This is a relational and identity problem, not merely a skin problem.
- "Napansin ng katrabaho ko hahahaha ngayon na conscious na ako." (My coworker noticed it, haha, now I'm conscious of it.)
Insight: The "hahahaha" is nervous deflection — the avatar is embarrassed that someone else noticed before they fully processed the problem themselves. External perception is a massive trigger. Being seen by others accelerates the internal shame spiral.
- "I wouldn't care as much if my entire body was just that singular dark color as I find darkskin beautiful, but the rest of my body doesn't match my face which makes me extremely insecure."
Insight: This is a nuanced and important distinction. The avatar is not engaging in colorism against themselves — they are distressed by the mismatch, the lack of uniformity. The desire isn't necessarily to be lighter; it's to be coherent. To look like one person.
- "I've grown out my stubble. Surprisingly I don't have acne in this photo but I break out regularly around my cheeks and lower jaw... Sometimes the skin is even, bright and looks great. Sometimes it's textured, dull and there's uneven tone."
Insight: The unpredictability of the skin adds a layer of anxiety. The avatar never knows which version of their face they'll wake up to. This cycle of good days and bad days keeps them perpetually searching for a permanent fix.

Insight: CORE INSIGHT. Treatments that cause side effects or worsen adjacent problems are quickly abandoned. The avatar's tolerance for "it gets worse before it gets better" is very low — especially when the worsening is visible to others.

## Objections
- "I refuse to bleach my skin (although I've thought about doing it) because of all the horror stories."
Insight: Fear of irreversible damage — particularly for darker and melanated skin tones — is a primary objection to aggressive treatments. Any solution that sounds too strong, chemical-heavy, or associated with "bleaching" will trigger immediate resistance, even if the formulation is benign.
- "I would be careful with things like hydroquinone and intense chemical peels, because they can cause hyperpigmentation rather than fade it."
Insight: The avatar has been burned (literally and figuratively) by treatments that backfired. The fear of making things worse is a stronger force than the hope of making things better. Safety and gentleness must be established before efficacy is even considered.
- "Suggestions will pour in, but how to use, what percentage to start with, how frequently to apply, and what to expect… nobody can accurately suggest these things because every skin is different."
Insight: The avatar is deeply skeptical of generic, one-size-fits-all advice. They've heard it before and it hasn't worked. Any solution that doesn't acknowledge the individuality of their skin tone, sensitivity, and lifestyle will be dismissed as "just another suggestion."
- "Di ko dn knows pano solusyunan huhu" (I don't even know how to solve this)
Insight: Overwhelm and confusion are objections in themselves. When an avatar doesn't know where to start, they don't start at all. The complexity of the skincare landscape — acids, percentages, layering orders — creates decision paralysis that keeps them stuck.
- "I've been using sunscreen for over 2 months now... but I only noticed that my face became duller."
Insight: Patience fatigue is a real objection. Two months of effort with negative visible results will cause most avatars to abandon a regimen entirely, even if the solution was working below the surface. Any product or protocol must deliver some visible early signal to retain trust.
- "the formula is oil based and I find it annoying to layer w other skincare so always forget to use it."
Insight: Friction kills follow-through. If a solution disrupts an established routine or requires extra steps, it will be deprioritized and forgotten — even when the avatar knows it works. Ease of integration is a make-or-break feature, not a nice-to-have.

## Level of Awareness
Level: Solution Aware (with a significant subset at Problem Aware)
The majority of this avatar clearly knows they have a problem (uneven skin tone, hyperpigmentation, face-body color mismatch) and has already identified and experimented with categories of solutions — sunscreen, vitamin C, azelaic acid, retinol, kojic soap, chemical peels. They are not asking "what is hyperpigmentation?" — they are asking "what actually works?" This is Solution Aware territory.
However, a meaningful sub-segment — particularly first-time posters and those from the Philippines/India subreddits — is still at Problem Aware. They've just noticed the problem, have no existing routine, and need both education and a clear first step.
Supporting evidence:
- "I have used chemical exfoliants in the past but I did not see much difference. I do use COSRX AHA BHA Toner sometimes. I use clay mask weekly for my big pores. Please help :("
Insight: This is a Solution Aware avatar who has tried multiple solutions, is frustrated with results, and is openly asking for something better. They know the category of solutions that should work — they just haven't found the right execution.
- "Wala po talaga ako alam sa skin care now lang naging conscious." (I really don't know anything about skincare, I've only just become conscious of it now.)
Insight: This is a Problem Aware avatar at the very beginning of their awareness journey — they know something is wrong but have zero framework for fixing it. They need to be met where they are with education-first messaging before any product recommendation will land.
- "I kept switching products every few weeks thinking something wasn't working. In reality, I just wasn't giving anything enough time."
Insight: This avatar has moved beyond Solution Aware into a more sophisticated understanding — recognizing that the failure was behavioral, not product-related. This sub-segment is closer to Product Aware and is ready for a specific, well-positioned solution.
