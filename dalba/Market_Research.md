# Avatar Deep Dive Research

## Pain Points
- "my skin looks kinda dull and flat, not glowy at all. I keep seeing this glass skin look everywhere and I honestly can't tell what's real and what's just lighting/filtering."
Insight: This person is not suffering from severe acne or disease — they're suffering from comparison. The pain isn't physical; it's perceptual. They're being gaslit by curated imagery and can no longer trust their own eyes to evaluate what "normal" skin looks like.
- "I feel like I have scarring left behind and that I have to use a bunch of acids and expensive treatments to get the smooth and even texture and complexion that I want."
Insight: The word "have to" is doing enormous psychological work here. This avatar feels obligated — almost punished — by their skin history. Acne left marks, and now they feel they must spend indefinitely to undo the past.
- "I keep seeing people with that insane glow, the kind of body skin that literally looks shiny and glowy… My skin has definitely become softer and smoother, but that radiant glow still feels out of reach."
Insight: CORE INSIGHT — Progress without arrival. They're doing the work, seeing incremental results, but the goalpost keeps moving. This creates a cycle of effort + disappointment that's deeply demoralizing and keeps them perpetually buying and trying.
- "I have bad blood circulation, when I was 16 I already got Varicose Veins and spider veins. Also horrible looking cellulite even though I was skinny and working out. Also water in my legs. I envy women with beautiful legs so much."
Insight: For some in this avatar group, the pain runs deeper than routine fixes can address. There's genuine grief here — a body that has failed them despite doing "everything right." The envy is raw and specific.
- "Glass skin is not real, it just an advertised thing to sell korean products and to very honest its just a Instagram filter. For smooth and porefree skin visit a dermatologist don't experiment with online products it will damage your skin barrier speaking from personal experience"
Insight: A subset of this avatar has been burned — their skin barrier has been damaged from chasing the trend. The pain isn't just aesthetic disappointment; it's the physical consequence of over-experimenting, which creates a new layer of fear and mistrust.
- "It fucked up my skin BAD. It got SO dry my skin would peel off, I had very serious acne like never before, my skin was dull etc."
Insight: This is a wound-story. This person followed advice, trusted products, and ended up in a worse position. The emotional charge here is betrayal — by the internet, by influencers, by products that promised and damaged.
- "social media has really caused some serious skin dysmorphia."
Insight: A growing segment of this avatar is waking up to the psychological manipulation embedded in the glass skin trend — but awareness doesn't automatically dissolve the desire. Knowing you've been conditioned doesn't make you immune.

## Day-to-Day Struggles
- "I can't have smooth base for makeup 😭. My skin texture seems off. It's got little bumps over it."
Insight: The struggle isn't just vanity — it's functional. Texture disrupts makeup application, which disrupts confidence in how they present themselves to the world daily. This creates a cascading morning frustration ritual.
- "The biggest factor for me is my diet. I only have skin like that when I'm not dieting. It's very frustrating as someone trying to lose weight."
Insight: This avatar is caught between two self-improvement goals that actively undermine each other. Skin health and weight loss are in direct conflict, and neither goal feels fully achievable at the same time. This is an unusually specific and painful double-bind.
- "I've been dealing with open pores for 10+ years. I've tried pretty much everything, and at this point I've accepted that they won't fully go away without procedures. That's just been my experience."
Insight: CORE INSIGHT — Chronic, unresolved frustration. Ten years is a long time to fight the same battle. This language signals exhaustion and forced acceptance rather than genuine peace. The word "accepted" is resigned, not empowered.
- "my skin always ends up looking oily instead of dewy. I've tried a few essences and toners but nothing seems to hit right."
Insight: The thin line between "dewy" and "greasy" is a daily torture for this avatar. They're not achieving the look they want — they're achieving a version that makes them feel worse. The result is the exact opposite of the goal, which compounds frustration.
- "When I'm eating everything my skin GLOWS" / "I feel like it's 90% tretinoin, dermaplaning... and LOTS of moisture"
Insight: CORE INSIGHT — This avatar has discovered that the variables controlling their skin extend far beyond the bathroom shelf. Diet, sleep, hormones, stress — all of it shows up on their face. This makes consistent results feel maddeningly out of their control.

## Victories
- "I've been getting compliments on my complexion since I focused on using certain ingredients and the products that have them there."
Insight: External validation is a primary success metric for this avatar. It's not enough to feel good themselves — they need other people to notice. This reveals that the deeper goal is social recognition, not private satisfaction.
- "My red light mask has helped a lot... within just a few weeks, my cheeks went from being somewhat mottled to very clear and bright. It's subtle, but I definitely noticed a difference."
Insight: The word "subtle" is important here — this avatar celebrates small, incremental wins. They've calibrated their expectations to be realistic, which means they're open to gradual improvement tools, not just overnight miracles.
- "i use tretinoin and his skin was glassy. My skin(colour of milk) is almost glassy I put it down to proper cleansing and exfoliation."
Insight: Those who found their "one thing" — whether tretinoin, a specific toner, or a technique — speak about it with the fervor of a convert. This represents the emotional payoff the entire avatar group is chasing: the moment of discovery that ends the searching.
- "Face hasn't been this clear and glowy in yearsss"
Insight: The time anchor ("years") tells us how long this avatar has been waiting for results. When success finally arrives, it's emotionally outsized — relief, pride, and disbelief all at once.
- "The one thing that made the biggest noticeable difference for me was adding in a milky toner to my morning routine. My skin is RADIANT throughout the day (and I keep getting compliments on it). Didn't change anything else from my routine except the toner."
Insight: CORE INSIGHT — The fantasy of the "one simple change" is deeply appealing to this audience. After the complexity and failure of elaborate routines, the idea that a single product switch can produce radiant skin + compliments is extraordinarily compelling.

## Failures
- "I was basically giving a try to what those social media influencers were saying... It fucked up my skin BAD. It got SO dry my skin would peel off, I had very serious acne like never before, my skin was dull etc."
Insight: Influencer-following is a documented failure mode for this avatar. They trusted authority figures who had no personal stakes in their individual skin type, and paid the price physically. This creates deep distrust of recommendation culture.
- "I've bought into buying a bunch of products hoping to get that glass skin effect but it's not as glassy or reflective if you have texture issues. So really the main issues need to be corrected first and the products are there to maintain."
Insight: CORE INSIGHT — Product-first thinking before addressing root issues is the most common and costly mistake. This avatar often treats symptoms rather than causes, leading to a graveyard of half-used products that didn't deliver as promised.
- "I used to try many skincare products. From L'Oréal to Korean brands. From very hydrating creams to various Acids... When I stopped using those products, my skin would get dry thick and horrible."
Insight: Over-reliance on products created a dependency — skin that could no longer function without intervention. The failure isn't just that products didn't work; it's that they may have trained the skin to need them. This is a quietly frightening outcome.
- "I've struggled with acne throughout most of my 20s and while the acne and regular breakouts are behind me, the texture of my skin bothers me. I feel like I have scarring left behind."
Insight: Winning the acne battle but losing the texture war is a specific failure state that creates its own grief. They did "everything right" to clear their acne, only to discover a new problem emerged from the process.
- "starting a month ago with 0.025% breaking out like crazy 😭"
Insight: Even when this avatar chooses a clinically validated solution (tretinoin), the purging phase creates doubt and abandonment risk. They fail not because the product doesn't work, but because they don't have the context to understand or endure the process.

This creates a trust vacuum that an honest brand could fill.
- "I feel like glow comes from the inside too — not just what you put on your face."
Insight: This belief reveals that this avatar is open to a holistic approach. They won't be fully satisfied by topical solutions alone — they're looking for a total-body framework that addresses diet, supplements, sleep, and stress.
- "Glass skin = skin without blemishes" / "Glass skin is often a combination of recently applied moisturizer or oil + good lighting. Don't beat yourself if you don't get that glow irl"
Insight: There's an active internal debate within this avatar community about what glass skin even means. Some believe it's achievable; others believe it's entirely fabricated. This ambiguity is a source of confusion and keeps them spinning between hope and resignation.
- "Good genes is such a buzzword" / "the most raw answer is... its genes... simply genes... you cannot get what's never been yours or of anyone's in your bloodline"
Insight: CORE INSIGHT — The genetics belief is both the most demoralizing and the most commonly held. When people believe the ceiling is genetic, they feel that effort is futile. A product or approach that credibly reframes what's achievable regardless of genetics would be enormously powerful.
- "Expensive products don't make a huge difference. Vitamin C and retinol are cheap."
Insight: This avatar is developing a more sophisticated consumer belief — price does not equal efficacy. They've been burned by expensive products that underperformed and have learned to prioritize active ingredients over branding.

## Objections
- "I heard that makes your face lighter? I heard about it but im kind of scared i don't want my melanin to be stripped lmao"
Insight: For darker-skinned users, there is a specific fear that Korean skincare products contain whitening/bleaching agents that will alter their natural skin tone. This is a legitimate barrier rooted in cultural awareness and real product concerns — not just paranoia.
- "I think you should account for climate and skin type too. I live in extremely low humidity. It takes a lot for me to get the glass/dewy skin look because there's no moisturizer in the air."
Insight: Context-dependency is a major objection. This avatar is skeptical of universal recommendations because they've been burned by advice that worked for someone in a different environment, with different skin, in a different climate. They need personalized, not generic, guidance.
- "With a 12 step skincare routine, even then it's not attainable for most people."
Insight: CORE INSIGHT — The complexity objection is huge. Many in this avatar group feel that achieving glass skin requires an overwhelming commitment of time, money, and expertise they don't have. The barrier isn't desire — it's perceived inaccessibility.
- "I don't have any procedures but probably would get some if I could afford them."
Insight: Cost is a significant barrier — particularly for clinical procedures. This avatar knows what works (tretinoin, microneedling, lasers) but may not be able to access it. Products that bridge the gap between drugstore and dermatologist hold enormous appeal.
- "Glass skin is a social media trend, which often involves a lot of filters and editing... Don't compare yourself to someone who's under 10 different filters."
Insight: Disillusionment functions as a self-protective objection. Some members of this avatar talk themselves out of chasing the goal by reframing it as fake — but this is often a coping mechanism masking genuine desire, not a resolved decision to stop trying.

## Level of Awareness
**Level: Solution Aware (trending toward Product Aware)**
This avatar knows they have a problem (dull, textured, uneven skin that doesn't match the "glass skin" ideal). They are deeply aware that solutions exist — they're actively researching retinoids, chemical exfoliants, Korean skincare routines, hydration techniques, and professional procedures. They can name specific actives (tretinoin, niacinamide, azelaic acid, vitamin C, hyaluronic acid) and specific procedures (microneedling, CO2 laser, chemical peels). What they lack is certainty about *which* solution is right for *their specific skin* — and they're exhausted by trial and error.
- "I wanted to ask: Are there any vitamins or supplements you've personally tried that actually helped with skin radiance? What skincare products give that glassy, hydrated look without causing breakouts? Any small habits or tips that made a noticeable difference? Would love to hear real experiences, not just hype."
Insight: This is textbook Solution Aware behavior. They're not asking "what is wrong with my skin?" — they're asking "which solution actually works?" The emphasis on "real experiences" signals distrust of marketing messaging and desire for peer verification.
- "I've bought into buying a bunch of products hoping to get that glass skin effect but it's not as glassy or reflective if you have texture issues. So really the main issues need to be corrected first and the products are there to maintain."
Insight: This person has cycled through product-awareness and back — they've tried products, failed, and are now reassessing the problem from a higher-order perspective. They're solution-aware but deeply skeptical, which requires copy that leads with honesty, not claims.

## Real Customer Language
- "glass skin" — Why this phrase matters: This is the aspirational shorthand the entire community rallies around. It's a visual metaphor — transparent, smooth, reflective — that communicates a specific aesthetic ideal in two words. Any product or brand in this space must speak this language fluently.
- "dull and flat" — Why this phrase matters: These two words are the baseline negative self-description. They don't say "bad skin" or "broken out" — they say their skin lacks dimension and radiance. This is the problem state in their own words, and copy should mirror it back to them.
- "skin barrier" — Why this phrase matters: CORE INSIGHT — This phrase appears across nearly every post in the dataset. It's become a central organizing concept for this avatar. Products that frame themselves around barrier health speak the language of the educated, modern skincare consumer.
- "purging phase" — Why this phrase matters: This shows sophistication — they know that some treatments cause skin to get worse before better. Brands that acknowledge this phase build trust immediately, because it signals they won't sugarcoat the process.
- "the glass skin look is mostly skincare + good layering. You can get that glazed, dewy finish with the right routine and will only last till your skincare is on." — Why this phrase matters: "Glazed, dewy finish" is the experiential language of what glass skin *feels* like to achieve — temporary, freshly-applied, luminous. Products that promise to extend or lock in this state address a real, named frustration.
- "real experiences, not just hype" — Why this phrase matters: CORE INSIGHT — This phrase (and variations of it) appears repeatedly. It's the north star of this community's content consumption. They've been burned by hype. They've learned to filter for authenticity. Copy that reads as genuine peer experience rather than marketing claim will outperform every time.
- "I feel I lost my glow" — Why this phrase matters: "Glow" as something that can be *lost* — owned, then taken away — gives it a personal, almost possession-like quality. This language unlocks a recovery narrative frame: not "get glow for the first time," but "get your glow back."

## Visual Cues
- "tretinoin makes my skin look like (in the words of charlotte palermino) the surface of an ice rink"
Insight: The "ice rink" metaphor is an extraordinarily powerful visual trigger — glassy, reflective, smooth, almost supernatural. Images that evoke this quality (wet, luminous, light-refracting skin) would immediately communicate the desired outcome to this audience.
- "my friends call it 'peeled egg skin'"
Insight: "Peeled egg skin" is a tactile and visual metaphor that communicates smoothness, softness, and a clean, almost naked quality. Images showing skin that looks fresh, unfiltered, and naturally dewy — not airbrushed — would resonate strongly with this audience's desire for achievable-looking results.
- "Your skin already looks glowy in the after photo. It would help if you exfoliate + shaved or waxed to achieve the smooth shiny look... a tan or darker complexion would enhance the glow."
Insight: The before/after visual format is extremely powerful for this audience, but it must be realistic — not dramatic or obviously filtered. Side-by-side comparisons that show achievable texture improvement under natural lighting will perform far better than studio-perfect imagery that triggers their media literacy and distrust.
- "You can get that glazed, dewy finish with the right routine... Also, I've been dealing with open pores for 10+ years."
Insight: Close-up skin texture photography — particularly showing pore minimization, smoothed texture, or improved radiance — acts as a direct visual trigger for this avatar's core anxiety. Images that show skin "before" looking like theirs, and "after" looking like the goal, close the believability gap.

## Trigger Moments
- "I feel I lost my glow. I switched from the clear blue cerave moisturizer bottle to the white bottle with the green on it? should I drop cerave completely?"
Insight: A product change that visibly worsened skin is a classic trigger moment — the sudden awareness that something is wrong pushes this avatar into active solution-seeking. They move from passive skincare routine maintenance to urgent research mode.
- "I'm pretty happy with how my skin looks but I rly want to get next level poreless glass skin... my acutane trial entirely cleared my skin and I rarely get acne anymore. However, I think it left some scars and marks on my face that are still slightly visible."
Insight: The end of one skin problem (acne) becomes the trigger for pursuing a new goal (glass skin). Milestone moments — finishing a treatment, clearing a condition, starting a new chapter — often activate the desire for "what's next." This is the "I've come this far, now I want more" trigger.
- "No no I just used this pic. I know it's not possible but I want clean clear skin 😭😭 atleast. Bare minimum."
Insight: CORE INSIGHT — Social comparison with filtered/idealized imagery is the most common and powerful trigger across the entire dataset. Seeing glass skin on social media — whether real or filtered — creates immediate dissatisfaction with their own skin and activates the search for solutions. The phrase "bare minimum" reveals the scale of adjustment this avatar has already made, and how desperately they want even partial results.
- "Glass skin has always been my ultimate goal and I think I'm pretty close."
Insight: Near-achievement is a powerful trigger for deeper investment. When this avatar senses they're close to their goal, they intensify their search for the final piece — the product, technique, or treatment that will close the last gap. This is the highest-intent purchase moment in the entire customer journey.
