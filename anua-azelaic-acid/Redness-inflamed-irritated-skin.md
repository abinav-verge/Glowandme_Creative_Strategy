# Avatar Deep Dive Research

## Pain Points
- "the acne mark/scar plus the redness is totally decaying my confidence"
Insight: The stacking of two visible skin issues — scarring and redness — creates a compounding emotional wound. "Decaying" is a visceral word that reveals how chronic skin issues slowly hollow out a person's self-image over time, not in a dramatic crash but in a slow, relentless erosion.
- "I have the same problem as you! It makes me really insecure, and the only thing that makes it a little less noticeable is makeup, which I hate wearing."
Insight: The avatar is caught in a trap: the solution (makeup) creates its own problem (feeling fake, masked, dependent). This isn't just about redness — it's about wanting to exist authentically without a layer of concealment between them and the world.
- "I would not say I get bullied but I do get teased/made fun of for it."
Insight: Even when the harassment is soft ("teasing"), it lands with enormous psychological weight. The careful linguistic distancing ("I would not say bullied") shows someone minimizing their pain while simultaneously being deeply wounded by it. This person has internalized shame around something entirely outside their control.
- "i'm a little older than you and had the same issue for years. I remember getting called a strawberry."
Insight: Social cruelty leaves a mark that outlasts the incident. This commenter is older, looking back — the nickname still sticks. It shows that childhood or social ridicule around facial redness creates lasting identity wounds that no moisturizer can address.
- "I hate it! My derm told me it was anxiety and blood pressure going up through the day - causes flushed pink face."
Insight: Being told the cause is anxiety adds a layer of shame — the body is now broadcasting an internal emotional state publicly. The flush becomes a visible confession of their inner world that they never chose to share.
- "I've had it since 18 and now 35... it's ruining my life."
Insight: CORE INSIGHT. Nearly two decades of living with this condition, and the language hasn't softened — it's still "ruining my life." This is not a minor inconvenience. It is a daily, relentless presence that has shaped this person's relationship with themselves and the world around them.
- "I stopped off at Shoppers to grab it on my way back from the gym but they were out... Need to get a better sunscreen. I despise the smell of sunscreen so need to find one that's moisturizing and doesn't smell and maybe tinted?"
Insight: Even well-meaning skincare routines create daily micro-frustrations. The quest isn't just for clear skin — it's for products that don't add new burdens (smell, feel, inconvenience). Every product is a negotiation.

## Day-to-Day Struggles
- "I have a feeling I have redness/rosacea. It still happens but very rarely now (if I take a long hot shower, eat spicy food, am stressed, etc.)."
Insight: CORE INSIGHT. The avatar must constantly negotiate their behavior around triggers — food choices, shower temperature, emotional reactions, environmental exposure. Ordinary human experiences (a hot shower, a spicy meal, a stressful meeting) have become landmines. Life is being quietly restructured around a skin condition.
- "my skin gradually gets redder throughout the day... my skin always looks uniform in colour when I first wake up. After a few hours... my skin starts to go pink."
Insight: The redness isn't static — it builds throughout the day, creating a deteriorating experience of one's own appearance. Mornings offer brief hope; afternoons deliver disappointment. This daily arc shapes mood, confidence, and social willingness.
- "I sometimes use a redness correcting primer (it's green), and I guess that's ok, but I'd rather my face just not be red."
Insight: Using a green-tinted primer is an admission of defeat disguised as a workaround. The phrase "I guess that's ok" is deeply resigned — it's the language of someone who has stopped hoping for a solution and is settling for camouflage. But the desire underneath is still burning: "I'd rather my face just not be red."
- "I woke up with red marks on my face... My skin was a bit itchy throughout the day, but wasn't unbearable. Although I also notice some small bumps."
Insight: This person is monitoring their skin obsessively — noting redness, bumps, itch levels — because their skin has become a daily diagnostic puzzle they can't stop solving. The constant self-surveillance is exhausting and anxiety-producing.
- "Cold season is the WORST for my skin for sure."
Insight: Environmental factors — wind, cold, temperature change — create predictable suffering that comes with the seasons. For sufferers of facial redness, even the weather is an enemy.
- "even the most simple and 'sensitive skin' products burned. I stopped literally everything except Vanicream for a few months."
Insight: When even "gentle" products cause pain, the entire framework of skincare — the thing supposed to help — becomes a source of fear and distress. The avatar has been abandoned by the solutions designed for them.

## Victories
- "I just grew out of it eventually I think."
Insight: For some, the passage of time — not products — was the answer. This creates both hope and helplessness in the community: hope that it may resolve, helplessness because there's nothing actively to do.
- "I believe I did end up seeing a dermo... I did end up getting my eczema under control with steroid cream and then I weaned off that with a cream from Avene called Cicalphate."
Insight: Medical intervention, combined with a thoughtful weaning process, produced the most lasting relief. The prescription pathway — despite its challenges — represents a real win when navigated properly.
- "The Ordinary Azelaic Acid 10% lotion... I think it has had a minor effect. I'm still red, but it has slightly diminished."
Insight: Even partial improvement is celebrated. The bar isn't perfection — it's "slightly diminished." This reveals how beaten-down the avatar is; a small win feels enormous when you've been losing for years.
- "switching from washing my face with hot water to cold alone helped my facial redness so much."
Insight: CORE INSIGHT. Simple behavioral changes — not products — have produced real wins. This is encouraging and frustrating simultaneously: the solution was free, but it took years to discover. It validates the power of eliminating irritants rather than adding solutions.
- "I've been using a 15% prescription azelaic acid cream for about 6 months now with good results, I mostly just struggle with redness around my nose and chin now."
Insight: Progress is real but partial. "Mostly just" struggling is a victory when you've been suffering broadly. Prescription-strength treatments represent the clearest path forward for this avatar.
- "I've been on two — Metrogel and Soolantra. The Metrogel stopped working for me but the Soolantra has been keeping my skin clear for about 5 or 6 years now."
Insight: Long-term management, not a cure, is the realistic aspiration. Five to six years of relative clarity is spoken of with deep gratitude, showing just how much this condition dominates when unmanaged.

## Failures
- "I tried several different cleansers... Unfortunately, each one of them irritated my skin."
Insight: CORE INSIGHT. The avatar has often been trapped in a cycle where every new attempt at repair causes further damage. Each failed product isn't just a waste of money — it's another assault on hope. The repeated failure pattern breeds profound learned helplessness.
- "I have tried suncreen, lotions, dermo antibac cream, prosaic cream, cereve, blue light block on monitor, decrease foods with b vitamins, stop Ibprofen, and several others, but no help. ALWAYS THE SAME."
Insight: "ALWAYS THE SAME" in all caps is a scream of exhaustion. This person hasn't been passive — they've been relentlessly experimenting across products, lifestyle factors, and hardware. The total lack of results after this effort produces a specific kind of despair.
- "I used tretinoin, which is supposed to help, but it's been a year and I haven't really seen an improvement."
Insight: Even gold-standard interventions can fail, and a year of no progress is a long time to hold onto hope. This undermines trust in expert recommendations and makes the avatar feel uniquely broken — like the treatments work for everyone except them.
- "Azelaic acid 10% gave me even more redness like a (sunburn) and dryness. Be aware if you have sensitive skin! I used it for a month without any improvements."
Insight: The most widely recommended solution for this community (azelaic acid) is itself a trigger for some. This creates paralysis: when the "safe" option isn't safe, what's left?
- "I got diagnosed with rosacea by several dermatologists and my skin has looked similar to yours in the past... It also tends to get worse as you get older."
Insight: Not only do current treatments fail — the condition is progressive. The prospect of worsening with age adds existential dread to an already frustrating situation. This is a villain with compounding interest.
- "The Ordinary lactic acid destroyed my skin barrier. I literally had to stop everything and go back to the basics."
Insight: CORE INSIGHT. Popular, trendy products touted in skincare communities have actually caused significant harm to these avatars. Trust in community-recommended products has been burned — literally. This creates a deep suspicion of anything "highly recommended."

## Goals

Insight: The goal isn't full perfection — it's reduction and evening. The avatar wants to look like a normal version of themselves, not a glamorized version. They want congruence, not transformation.
- "I want to stop wearing makeup but there's 2 things stopping me; my face gets red like this and the skin under my eyes is so thin."
Insight: The deepest desire is freedom from concealment. Makeup isn't aspirational for this avatar — it's a prison. The goal is bare-faced confidence, not beauty through product.
- "I just want to know what products help reduce this (not makeup, unless that's a valid option)."
Insight: CORE INSIGHT. The avatar actively rejects cosmetic cover-up as a solution. They want a fix, not a mask. This is critical: they're not looking for a better way to hide — they're looking for a way to not need to hide.
- "hopefully this helps, I'll be doing research for good non expensive lotions or creams to help with this as well."
Insight: Accessibility and affordability matter enormously. This isn't someone who can (or wants to) spend luxury money on skincare. They want something that works and doesn't break the bank. Price is a real objection.
- "I would like to go to a dermatologist next year and see what they actually say, maybe a prescription dilution could have a stronger effect."
Insight: The avatar knows the OTC path has a ceiling. They're beginning to orient toward medical intervention, but barriers (cost, access, time) keep delaying the step that might actually help them.
- "I hope you can get the help you need. Your skin is not a reflection of who you are."
Insight: What this commenter is offering — and what the avatar deeply craves — is the dissociation of their skin from their identity. They don't want to be defined by their redness. The goal isn't just clear skin; it's reclaiming authorship of who they are.

## Beliefs
- "I am adamant it has to be something to do with gut health more than what I apply."
Insight: CORE INSIGHT. A significant segment of this community believes the problem is internal — not topical. They've lost faith in surface-level solutions and have begun looking inward to diet, gut health, and systemic inflammation. This is a deeply held belief that creates resistance to purely topical product recommendations.
- "I just feel like it isn't rosacea from what I've seen. My face is more flushed, as if I had just been exercising. Maybe high blood pressure?"
Insight: The avatar is doing their own amateur diagnostics — and frequently rejecting the most obvious diagnosis (rosacea). This denial delays appropriate treatment and keeps them searching for alternative explanations. It's not stubbornness; it's the result of being dismissed by doctors and receiving too many contradictory opinions.
- "I just feel like if I could reduce carbs and sugar significantly, I'd see a difference since they all worsen inflammation. But it's so hard to do so."
Insight: The avatar suspects their own diet and lifestyle choices are contributing to their skin — and they feel guilty about not changing them. This creates a layer of self-blame layered on top of an already self-conscious relationship with their face.
- "i'm beginning to think it has to do with our gut health. I wonder the results after a serious detox/cleanse over the span of a few months but who tf has the mental capacity for all that."
Insight: The gut-skin connection is a widely-held belief in this community, but the solution feels overwhelming. The avatar believes in the theory but feels too exhausted and depleted to execute on it. Belief without capacity creates guilt and inaction.
- "Doctors are not always correct."
Insight: Medical authority has been eroded in this community. Too many people have received dismissive, wrong, or unhelpful diagnoses. This creates both skepticism of medical advice and an over-reliance on Reddit strangers — a dangerous but understandable swap.
- "I never went to a dermatologist to get diagnosed, but I have a feeling I have rosacea."
Insight: CORE INSIGHT. Self-diagnosis is rampant. A large portion of this community is operating without a confirmed diagnosis, treating guesses with guesses. This represents both a problem (incorrect treatment) and an opportunity (the right authoritative guidance would be deeply welcomed).

## Objections
- "I was debating it for a while (because $$) but I'm so sick of it."
Insight: Cost is a real and recurring barrier to seeking dermatological care. The avatar knows the right path (see a derm, get a prescription) but financial pressure delays action. They'll suffer longer to avoid the bill — until the suffering becomes unbearable enough.
- "Seeing one has been on my mind… was hoping I could avoid it."
Insight: Avoidance isn't laziness — it's the accumulated weight of previous disappointments. If doctors have dismissed them before ("just embrace the redness," "just use sensitive skin products"), why would this time be different? Hope has a cost, and they're protecting what little they have left.
- "I don't think it's a reaction. I've been using this routine for some time."
Insight: Familiarity creates blind spots. Because they've been using the same products for a long time, they don't consider those products as potential causes. This is a critical objection because it means they may resist recommendations that require abandoning beloved routine staples.
- "I tried the moisturizer once and immediately it burned. Which made me sad as I love the face wash."
Insight: CORE INSIGHT. Emotional attachment to existing products creates real resistance to change. The avatar isn't just switching products — they're grieving the loss of something that worked, or was familiar, or gave them hope. New products carry the risk of new disappointment.
- "i'm at a loss here" / "I feel like giving up."
Insight: Learned helplessness is perhaps the deepest objection of all. After a parade of failed solutions, the avatar has begun to believe that nothing will work for them specifically. Any new product or recommendation must first overcome this resignation before it can overcome the redness.
- "My skin has always been on the dryer side, but now it's SO dry... I don't think it's a reaction."
Insight: The avatar often underestimates how much their current routine is contributing to their problem. They've normalized the damage. "I've changed nothing" is stated repeatedly — but the products they consider normal may be the exact cause of their escalating symptoms. This blind spot must be addressed gently.

## Level of Awareness
Classification: Problem Aware (with a significant subset moving toward Solution Aware)
The vast majority of this data shows people who are acutely, painfully aware that they have a problem — but they're confused about what's causing it, inconsistently diagnosed, and cycling through solutions without a coherent framework. They know their face is red. They know it's affecting their life. But they don't know whether it's rosacea, sensitive skin, a damaged barrier, histamine intolerance, a gut issue, or something else entirely — and this diagnostic fog keeps them stuck.
- "A dermatologist has told me it's not rosacea, and I just have sensitive skin. That is true, I do have sensitive skin, but there's got to be something I can do to help it, right?"
Insight: This person has been told a (possibly incomplete) diagnosis and given inadequate guidance. They're problem-aware and beginning to look for solutions — but they've been failed by the medical system, so they're turning to community. They're at the threshold between Problem Aware and Solution Aware, but the door to real solutions hasn't been opened for them yet.
- "i'm not sure if it's just how my skin is"
Insight: Some users aren't even fully problem-aware — they're questioning whether their "problem" is actually a problem, or just their baseline. This ambiguity means some in this audience need to be first convinced that their redness is treatable before they'll pursue any solution.
- "I've struggled for years with a lot of redness in my face, and I'm not sure if it's just how my skin is."
Insight: This is the diagnostic no-man's-land that defines this avatar's level of awareness: they've suffered long enough to know something is wrong, but not long enough (or with enough expert guidance) to know what to do about it. The job of any solution-provider is to close this gap clearly and compassionately.

## Real Customer Language
- "my face is always red" — Why this phrase matters: This is the title phrase that appears across multiple posts because it perfectly captures the defining quality of their experience: constancy. It's not occasional redness. It's a permanent state of being. Any copy that leads with this phrase will create an immediate mirror moment.
- "decaying my confidence" — Why this phrase matters: This is not standard beauty language. "Decaying" is visceral, dark, biological — it speaks to a slow consumption of self rather than a dip in mood. Copy using language of decay and restoration will land far harder than language of "boost" or "enhance."
- "I feel like giving up" — Why this phrase matters: This phrase signals that the avatar is at a crisis point of learned helplessness. It's the emotional state just before either complete abandonment OR desperate receptivity to a solution that can credibly promise to be different from everything they've already tried.
- "back to normal" — Why this phrase matters: The aspiration isn't perfection or transformation — it's restoration. This avatar had a "normal" once, and they want it back. Copy should speak to return and reclamation, not upgrade or enhancement.
- "I just want my face to not be red" — Why this phrase matters: The blunt simplicity of this desire — stripped of any aspiration for beauty, youth, or glow — reveals how far past marketing fantasies this person has traveled. They don't want to be glowing. They don't want compliments. They just want the redness to stop. Copy that acknowledges this directness will feel like relief.
- "it stings / it burns" — Why this phrase matters: Physical sensation language (stinging, burning) appears repeatedly and carries enormous weight. The skin has become a source of active pain, not just cosmetic concern. Products that promise "calming" or "soothing" need to speak directly to this sensory experience.
- "I should go to a derm but I was hoping to avoid it" — Why this phrase matters: This reveals the internal negotiation that characterizes this avatar's relationship with professional help. They know what they should do. They're avoiding it. Copy that removes the perceived barrier to professional-grade solutions (whether that's cost, access, or past disappointment) speaks directly to this common avoidance pattern.

## Trigger Moments
- "It all started when I decided to try out the Round Lab Dokdo cleanser, and this product irritated THE HECK out of my skin... yellow crust started to form on my skin."
Insight: A single product — often something trendy or community-recommended — becomes a catastrophic trigger event that destroys the skin barrier and launches a months-long spiral. This crisis moment creates urgency to find something definitive, not experimental. They're not browsing — they're searching for rescue.
- "I had clear skin til my late 20s, now early 30s."
Insight: The onset of redness in the early-to-mid 30s without clear cause is a major trigger event for this avatar. The before/after of their own life — clear skin then, red skin now — creates a specific urgency and grief that makes them actively seek answers. A product or service that speaks to the "sudden onset in your 30s" experience would trigger instant recognition.
- "I get, I would not say I get bullied but I do get teased/made fun of for it."
Insight: Social ridicule — even gentle "teasing" — is a decisive trigger that turns a manageable inconvenience into an urgent problem requiring action. The moment the skin condition attracts social commentary is the moment the search for a solution intensifies dramatically.
- "I've been dealing with some major skin issues for the past few months, and I'm feeling frustrated and hopeless."
Insight: The accumulation of repeated failure — multiple products tried, multiple doctors seen, no resolution — eventually tips into crisis. This is a trigger of exhaustion: when the effort-to-result ratio becomes intolerable, the avatar becomes maximally receptive to anything that genuinely promises a new approach.
- "It only worked for two months then it will come back again."
Insight: The pattern of temporary relief followed by relapse is a recurring trigger moment that re-activates the search. Every time the redness returns after a period of improvement, the avatar re-enters the market with renewed urgency and slightly higher desperation. Timing messaging around the relapse cycle (not just the initial discovery) would capture this avatar at peak receptivity.
