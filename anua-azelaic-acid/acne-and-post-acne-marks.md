# Avatar Deep Dive Research

## Pain Points
- "I've had these stubborn acne scars on my left cheek for months, and they just won't fade."
Insight: The word "stubborn" is doing enormous emotional work here — it signals a perceived battle of wills between the person and their skin. The scars aren't just there; they're *refusing* to leave. This language reveals a sense of powerlessness and mounting frustration at something that feels defiant.
- "I went through a horrible hormonal imbalance that ruined my skin. Prior to this, I always had clear glowy skin, never struggled with acne. I am turning 30 this December and this is my birthday gift I guess. =("
Insight: This is a grief response. The person is mourning a former version of themselves — their "clear glowy skin" self — and attaching that loss to a milestone birthday. The sarcasm ("birthday gift I guess") masks real devastation. This isn't just a skin problem; it's an identity crisis tied to aging and lost control.
- "Every red mark you see was once a deep, red, inflamed pimple that turned into a bright red mark after it was healed. At first I tried salicylic acid to try to help with the healing process and prevent a mark, but they appeared regardless of what spot treatment I used. This didn't use to happen and it's really hurting my self esteem."
Insight: The phrase "this didn't use to happen" is the emotional core here. This person is experiencing a before-and-after rupture in their self-image. The marks aren't just cosmetic — they're physical evidence of something going wrong that they can't stop, and the helplessness is eroding their confidence in real time.
- "my issue started with 1 in june and now i have 4 in september, is there really a point in spending months resolving these marks when I can just keep getting them? 🥲"
Insight: This reveals a deeper layer of despair — not just frustration with slow results, but a creeping hopelessness about whether healing is even worth pursuing. The upside-down smiley is the emoji equivalent of a quiet scream. This person is losing faith in the process entirely.
- "I've spent 5 figures on trying to get rid of my acne scars 🥲"
Insight: This single comment is a bombshell. Five figures spent and still seeking answers — this signals that for a segment of this audience, financial commitment is not the barrier. The barrier is finding something that *actually delivers*. The 🥲 emoji communicates exhausted resignation, not anger.
- "Repeating every single form of atrophic scar is permanent unless clinically treated causes much distress, body dysmorphia and might result in people engaging erratic and over-the-top invasive procedures."
Insight: This reveals a secondary layer of pain: the *community itself* is causing harm. Being told repeatedly that scars are permanent triggers anxiety spirals, BDD-adjacent thinking, and reckless spending. The avatar isn't just suffering from their skin — they're suffering from the narrative around their skin.
- "I know they will heal naturally but my face looks very bad (I never had acne until my recent stupid activity)"
Insight: The self-blame embedded in "my recent stupid activity" shows this person is carrying guilt on top of their physical problem. They didn't just get acne — in their mind, they *caused* it through ignorance. This guilt intensifies the emotional weight of every mark on their face.

## Day-to-Day Struggles
- "It's just annoying putting on foundation to go out anywhere ☹️"
Insight: Foundation has become a coping mechanism — a daily ritual of concealment. This isn't vanity; it's a survival strategy. The fact that they can't leave the house without it signals that the scars have quietly colonized their sense of freedom and spontaneity.
- "I have acne marks (PIE) on my cheeks and right above my lips. No matter how I do my makeup, it looks like my lipstick is crooked. Or that I have a herpes blister or something. Ugh!"
Insight: This is viscerally specific and deeply embarrassing. The comparison to a herpes blister shows how hyper-aware — and how harshly self-critical — these people are. Makeup, the supposed solution, is failing them. The marks are winning even the battles they try to fight.
- "I'm still trying to break the subconscious cycle of picking at my face."
Insight: This reveals a behavioral loop that perpetuates the problem. The scars cause distress, the distress drives picking, and the picking creates more scars. This is a psychological trap — not just a skincare issue — and it shows how deeply the problem is rooted in anxious self-monitoring.
- "it takes some months to see noticeable effects"
Insight: The slow timeline of results is itself a daily struggle. Every morning they look in the mirror and see no change. Patience isn't just a virtue here — it's a daily emotional tax. The length of the healing process creates a sustained psychological burden that drains motivation and compounds despair.
- "I can't get past the peeling."
Insight: Even when solutions exist, the side effects of those solutions (peeling, purging, burning) create daily discomfort that acts as a barrier to consistency — the very thing that determines success. The treatment becomes its own problem, trapping people in an impossible catch-22.

## Victories
- "They have gone COMPLETELY! ...You can't even tell where the previous scars were or that I ever had them to begin with. I've gotten so many compliments, especially from family, thinking I have makeup on and I don't. It's been so great!"
Insight: The emotional peak of this quote is "thinking I have makeup on and I don't." This is the fantasy made real — skin so healed it looks *effortless*. Social validation from family is a powerful marker of success for this avatar. This is the proof they're looking for.
- "those marks aren't 100% gone but they are 80% faded now!"
Insight: 80% improvement is being celebrated as a genuine victory. This tells us the bar isn't absolute perfection — it's meaningful, visible progress. This avatar can be satisfied with *significant* improvement, not just total elimination. That's a critical insight for messaging: promise transformation, not perfection.
- "Daily sunscreen has changed my skin texture and scars. Your change/improvement looks incredible, keep it up."
Insight: One of the simplest, cheapest habits — daily SPF — is being reported as a genuine game-changer. This validates that consistency with foundational habits produces visible results, and it gives hope to those who haven't started yet.
- "I have been SHOCKED by how effective azelaic acid has been for my skin... It's cheap, gentle, and perhaps the most effective product I've ever used for anything ever."
Insight: The word "SHOCKED" is the key emotional signal. This person wasn't expecting it to work — which means their baseline expectation is disappointment. When something actually delivers, the reaction is almost disbelief. Surprise and relief are major emotional drivers in this space.
- "Micro-needling is what helped the most for me"
Insight: For users who've cycled through topicals without resolution, finding a procedure that actually works produces a moment of breakthrough relief. This represents the "I finally found the answer" emotional milestone — a powerful state to speak to in copy.

## Failures
- "I tried using The Ordinary Peeling Solution for about a month, but I didn't see any noticeable results."
Insight: The Ordinary is one of the most recommended and popular affordable skincare brands — and it still failed to deliver. This signals that even "highly recommended" products frequently disappoint. Brand reputation does not equal personal results, and this avatar knows it firsthand.
- "Things that didn't help: Niacinamide, Tranexamic Acid. Things that Barely Helped: Alpha Arbutin/ Beta Arbutin, Soy."
Insight: This methodical logging of failures reveals a sophisticated, experimental mindset. This avatar has been treating their face like a lab, and yet the results remain elusive. The frustration here isn't ignorance — it's exhaustion from doing everything *right* and still failing.
- "I've tried everything for acne. Proactive, Ulta skincare shit my girlfriend made me buy, even random Amazon and bunk ass tik tok shop 'miracle creams' that did nothing."
Insight: The language here — "shit my girlfriend made me buy," "bunk ass tik tok shop miracle creams" — is dripping with cynicism born from repeated disappointment. This person is now deeply skeptical of mainstream recommendations and social media marketing. To reach them, you must first earn their trust.
- "I gave it a long shot with adapalene or tretinoin and my skin couldn't tolerate them"
Insight: Even the gold-standard clinical treatments have failed for some. Skin sensitivity and intolerance have blocked people from the most effective known solutions. This creates a desperate sub-segment looking for *gentler* alternatives that still produce real results.
- "I used this in the past but I didn't see much progress, not because of the product but rather I was in the thick of hormonal disaster"
Insight: This reveals that timing and root cause matter enormously. A product that failed once may not actually be a bad product — but that nuance is rarely communicated. Failed attempts due to underlying hormonal issues leave people feeling defeated by solutions that simply weren't applied to the right problem yet.
- "Accutane was the worst decision I ever made...Long term side effects that mimics Sjogrens. My life has been hell."
Insight: The nuclear option — Accutane — has devastated at least one person in this data set. "My life has been hell" is not hyperbole; it's a permanent quality-of-life destruction. This represents the fear that pushes people away from aggressive interventions and toward gentler, safer alternatives.

## Goals

Insight: There's a two-phase goal structure here: first stop the acne, then erase the evidence. Once the active problem is managed, the emotional focus shifts entirely to the aftermath — the scars. This is a signal for product positioning: speak to those who've conquered the acne but are now battling what it left behind.
- "Does it get better? Am I screwed for life?"
Insight: This raw, two-sentence plea reveals the most fundamental goal of all: hope. Not a specific product. Not a routine. Just the reassurance that their skin has a future. The goal isn't just clear skin — it's permission to believe that clear skin is still possible for them.
- "I can't wait to save enough money to get pico layering to get most of the stubborn ones."
Insight: This person is financially planning around their skin goals — saving up specifically for a treatment. This signals extreme motivation and a willingness to invest significantly when they believe something will work. The goal isn't "find a cheap fix" — it's "find the thing that actually works, no matter what."
- "I intend to ultimately end up using 0.1 every day for max results"
Insight: This reveals an optimization mindset — not just healing, but maximizing outcomes. A segment of this audience isn't satisfied with "good enough." They want the absolute best version of their skin possible. This is a high-commitment avatar who responds to efficacy claims and clinical-grade solutions.

## Beliefs
- "Of course, there is little research on this subject because who wants to fund a study which shows skin might heal on its own, when the treatments are highly lucrative?"
Insight: A significant portion of this audience believes the skincare and dermatology industries are financially motivated to keep them dependent on expensive treatments. This anti-establishment skepticism is a major psychological filter through which all product recommendations are evaluated. Any solution that feels "too commercial" will trigger this alarm.
- "I feel like over-promotion of invasive treatments in the literature might have to do with the financial interest behind them, and that is influencing skincare enthusiasts into pushing the idea that they are the only route to go about atrophic scarring."
Insight: CORE INSIGHT. This belief that the industry profits from fear and pessimism — deliberately overstating severity to push expensive treatments — is deeply held and widely shared. It creates a crisis of trust in experts, influencers, and brands alike. Brands that lead with transparency and education will have a distinct advantage.
- "And it didn't help that some family members told me to be prepared for it to never heal."
Insight: Negative belief systems are being implanted by the people closest to them — family. This makes the battle psychological as much as physical. They're fighting their own mind, their family's skepticism, *and* their skin simultaneously. Validating that healing *is* possible is a powerful counter-narrative.
- "Scars can only be faded by laser treatment"
Insight: A common absolutist belief circulating in these communities — one that's factually incorrect for many scar types but causes people to either give up on topicals prematurely or spend thousands unnecessarily. This belief creates a market opportunity for products that can demonstrably challenge it with before/after evidence.
- "Skin is a complex organ that is not yet fully known, it will evolve throughout your life and so will science."
Insight: A hopeful, science-forward belief held by a more nuanced segment of this audience. These people are open to emerging solutions, not just established protocols. They believe in biological potential and the body's capacity to change — which makes them receptive to innovative approaches.

## Objections
- "I'm so scared that I'll have a negative reaction to the laser treatment for some reason! (probably just me being worried and getting in my own head)"
Insight: Fear of side effects — especially from invasive treatments — is a real and present objection. Even when the person knows rationally their fear may be irrational, it still stops them from acting. Any solution must address safety concerns head-on, not dismiss them.
- "I wasn't willing to risk potential long-term side effects or mess with fertility"
Insight: Long-term health consequences are a higher-order objection that supersedes even the desire for clear skin. When the perceived cost is health — not just money — many people will choose to live with their scars. Safety and naturalness of a solution must be proactively established.
- "I'm broke rn, I'll have to save up to visit a dermat"
Insight: Cost is a genuine, practical objection — especially for younger users. Dermatologist visits, prescription treatments, and clinical procedures are financially inaccessible to a large portion of this audience. Affordable options with clinical-grade credibility occupy a highly valuable market position.
- "I thought I could not tolerate [azelaic acid] before because it burns."
Insight: Past negative experiences with specific ingredients create anticipatory resistance. Even effective solutions face rejection because of prior discomfort. Messaging must acknowledge and address the "it burned me before" objection by differentiating formulation, concentration, or application method.
- "it takes a good while, like 4+ weeks" / "if it takes a month to notice a difference, how long does it take to actually fade? 😭😭"
Insight: CORE INSIGHT. Time is a massive objection. This audience has been burned by slow results before and has lost patience. The expectation of a long timeline actively discourages starting. Any solution must either offer a credible speed advantage or reframe the timeline in a way that feels manageable and worth it.
- "Besides, invasive treatments are extremely costly, not effective 100% of the time and can leave very bad side effects."
Insight: The risk/reward calculation of clinical treatments is actively being questioned. This isn't just a cost objection — it's a value objection. If a treatment is expensive, painful, risky, AND not guaranteed to work, the perceived value collapses entirely.

## Level of Awareness
Level: **Solution Aware** (with a significant segment at Product Aware)
These users are past the point of simply knowing they have a problem. They've diagnosed their issue with surprising precision (PIH, PIE, atrophic scarring, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation) and are actively researching and testing solutions. They know about retinoids, azelaic acid, vitamin C, SPF, microneedling, and laser treatments. They're comparing modalities, asking for specific product recommendations, and sharing detailed protocols.
However, most of them have NOT found a product or approach they fully trust or that has delivered complete results. They are searching for *the* solution — the one that works for *their* specific situation. This makes them Solution Aware shading into Product Aware for those who've already started comparing specific brands (Differin vs tret, Cos de Baha vs Finacea, etc.).
- "hydroquinone (not for more than 3 months), azelaic acid, vitamin C, bakuchiol, retinols, and niacinamide can all help fade dark spots. The most potent would be hydroquinone (first) and azelaic acid as they interrupt the process of creating pigmentation."
Insight: This is not the language of someone who is Unaware or even Problem Aware. This is sophisticated, ingredient-level knowledge. The audience has done their homework. Copy that explains *what* PIH is will bore them — copy that tells them *which specific solution works faster and why* will hook them.
- "Things that didn't help: Niacinamide, Tranexamic Acid. Things that Barely Helped: Alpha Arbutin/ Beta Arbutin, Soy. Helped More: Kojic Acid. Helped Best: Hydroquinone, 20% Azelaic acid..."
Insight: This is a systematic, ranked evaluation of solutions — the behavior of someone deep in the Solution Aware stage who is running their own informal clinical trials. Reaching this person requires differentiation and specificity, not education about the problem.

## Real Customer Language
- "stubborn acne scars" — Why this phrase matters: "Stubborn" is one of the most repeated descriptors across the data. It personifies the scars as a willful opponent — something that *refuses* to respond. Copy that acknowledges the stubbornness of the problem will immediately resonate as "finally, someone who gets it."
- "won't fade" — Why this phrase matters: The present tense futility here is powerful. Not "haven't faded yet" — but "won't fade," as if the scars have made a decision. This language reveals a feeling of battling something with its own agenda. Using this phrase in headlines signals deep empathy.
- "ruined my skin" — Why this phrase matters: "Ruined" is catastrophic language — it implies something formerly good has been permanently destroyed. This tells us the emotional register is loss and damage, not just inconvenience. Any copy that echoes the "before/after" narrative of a ruined-then-restored skin story will land hard.
- "actually work" / "what ACTUALLY helped" — Why this phrase matters: CORE INSIGHT. The word "actually" appears in multiple post titles and comments as an emphasis on the gap between promises and reality. This avatar has been let down so many times that "actually works" has become a loaded, almost sarcastic demand. Lead with proof, not promises.
- "Am I screwed for life?" — Why this phrase matters: This is the existential fear underneath every post in this data set. It's dramatic, raw, and real. Headlines or hooks that address this fear directly — "No, you're not screwed for life. Here's what your skin is actually capable of" — will stop the scroll immediately.
- "It's really hurting my self esteem" — Why this phrase matters: Self-esteem is named explicitly — not "I feel less attractive" or "I hate how I look," but "my self esteem." This is the avatar naming the real wound. It gives brands permission to speak to confidence and self-worth directly, without feeling presumptuous.
- "Clear skin" — Why this phrase matters: Used aspirationally and as a marker of a former identity ("I always had clear glowy skin"). "Clear skin" is shorthand for a whole constellation of feelings: ease, confidence, freedom, normalcy. It's the promised land. Any product that credibly links itself to "clear skin" is speaking directly to the core desire.

## Visual Cues
- "thinking I have makeup on and I don't"
Insight: The image of someone being *mistaken for wearing makeup* when they're bare-faced is the aspirational visual this avatar craves. Before/after images where the "after" looks effortlessly luminous — not just less scarred, but genuinely glowing — will trigger the deepest desire response.
- "I can walk around shirtless"
Insight: The visual of uninhibited physical freedom — at a pool, at the beach, in summer — is a powerful trigger image for this audience. Showing real people doing ordinary things confidently, without concealing their skin, communicates the emotional outcome more powerfully than any close-up skin texture shot.
- "my marks are nowhere near that red"
Insight: Side-by-side comparison photos — particularly showing the *reduction of redness* rather than just texture improvement — function as irrefutable social proof in this community. Before/after images that capture the color change (angry red → skin-tone) trigger immediate credibility and desire in a way that descriptive copy alone cannot.
- "Skin is tighter, more even tone"
Insight: The visual language of "even tone" is aspirational shorthand in this community. Imagery that depicts skin with smooth, uniform coloration (not necessarily perfect, but consistent) triggers both hope and desire. Uneven, blotchy, red-against-pale-skin imagery triggers the fear side — use this contrast deliberately.
- "You have seriously amazing progress here"
Insight: Community reactions to progress photos — particularly the visible shock and celebration — function as visual social proof. Images that capture the *reaction* to a transformation (comments, upvotes, peer validation) are as powerful as the transformation itself. Screenshots of real community responses can be extraordinarily persuasive.

## Trigger Moments
- "I went through a horrible hormonal imbalance that ruined my skin. Prior to this, I always had clear glowy skin, never struggled with acne. I am turning 30 this December and this is my birthday gift I guess. =("
Insight: A significant hormonal event — PCOS diagnosis, thyroid disorder, coming off birth control, entering a new decade — triggers a crisis moment where the avatar *must* take action. Milestone birthdays amplify urgency. This is the moment they go from passive sufferer to active solution-seeker.
- "2 months ago used glyco excessively on my face, which led to damaged barrier, didn't know about it and continued using salicylic acid and niacinamide on face which led to more damage"
Insight: A self-inflicted skincare mistake — over-exfoliation, wrong product combination, ignoring warning signs — acts as a shock trigger. The moment they realize *they caused this* is a galvanizing moment of guilt and urgency. They immediately go looking for a solution because they feel personally responsible for fixing it.
- "my issue started with 1 in june and now i have 4 in september"
Insight: Watching the problem *grow and multiply* over months is a tipping point trigger. The creeping accumulation of new marks despite trying to prevent them transforms frustration into urgency. When the problem is visibly worsening, the motivation to find a real solution spikes dramatically.
- "I used to pay $40 a session at a wellness clinic just to sit under a red light. Scheduling sucked, prices added up and I hated the front desk lady"
Insight: The moment when an existing solution becomes too inconvenient, expensive, or unpleasant to continue is a trigger moment to seek an alternative. Pain of the current solution — not just the original problem — drives new product searches. This is the "I need a better way" trigger.
- "not because of the product but rather I was in the thick of hormonal disaster lol recently diagnosed with hypothyroid and PCOS"
Insight: A new medical diagnosis — particularly a hormonal one — reframes the skin problem and triggers a fresh search for solutions. "I finally understand *why* this is happening" is a catalyst moment that restores motivation and opens receptivity to new approaches. People who've just been diagnosed with a hormonal condition are primed to start over.
